# Policies and support

## Late work

The rule: work submitted up to five working days after its deadline is
accepted with no penalty and no form. Past five days, email to arrange a
new date before the original deadline passes, not after.

### Amendments to this policy

**v1 (2019).** Late work is accepted within five working days, no
questions asked.

**v2 (2021).** Late work is accepted within five working days, no
questions asked. Students seeking an extension beyond five days should
email the convenor with a reason before the original deadline.

**v3 (2023).** Late work is accepted within five working days without
penalty, subject to the standard grace-period provision (§2.1). Extension
requests beyond the grace period require a written request, submitted
before the original deadline, stating the reason and the requested new
date, to be assessed against the university's extension criteria.

**v4 (2025, current).** Late work falls within the standard grace-period
allowance of five (5) working days from the published deadline, applied
automatically and without academic-integrity implication, per the
grace-period provision (§2.1, as amended). Requests for an extension
beyond the grace-period allowance must be submitted in writing prior to
the expiry of the original deadline, must specify the substantive reason
for the request and the proposed revised date, and will be assessed
against the university's standard extension criteria (see §2.1.4) by the
convenor or their delegate.

Read v1 and v4 side by side. The rule is the same five days it was in
2019. Only the number of words required to say so changed.

## Extensions

If you need more than the five-day grace period above, email the convenor
before the deadline with a reason and a proposed new date. Extensions are
granted on the strength of the reason, not the length of the email.

## Academic integrity

Cite what isn't yours, including material another student's tools
produced with them. The course's own repeated re-wording of one idea (see
above) is a demonstration, not a licence — your submissions still need to
be your own analysis, stated once, correctly.

## Getting help

Ask in session first; it is the fastest feedback loop the course has.
Email the address on your teacher's [People](/people/) page for anything
that cannot wait for the next one.
